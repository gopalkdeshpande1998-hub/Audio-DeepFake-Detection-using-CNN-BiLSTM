-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THE WEBSITE IS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VoiceGuard is a local "audio forensics workstation" built with Streamlit. You give it a
voice recording and it answers three questions:

  a) IS IT REAL OR SYNTHETIC?   A two-way verdict, REAL or SYNTHETIC (AI-generated or
                                voice-converted speech).
  b) WHERE?                     Which seconds of the recording look synthetic.
  c) HOW FAR CAN I TRUST IT?    How often verdicts like this one were right on held-out
                                test data that the model never saw during training.

Everything runs on your own computer (GPU if available, otherwise CPU). No audio is
uploaded anywhere; files stay in memory for the browser session only.

The detector is ONE model, "E7a":
  WavLM-Base+ (a large pretrained speech model, fine-tuned, with learned layer weights)
  -> SE-ResNet CNN -> BiLSTM -> attentive statistics pooling -> P(fake).
  About 96.2 million parameters. Trained on ASVspoof 2019 LA train; thresholds and
  calibration were chosen on ASVspoof 2019 LA dev only.
(An older 3-model ensemble was retired on 2026-11-23 and is kept only as a research record.)


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. HOW IT WORKS (PIPELINE)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1  INPUT AND DECODING
        Audio (WAV, FLAC, MP3, OGG, M4A, AIFF, WebM, Opus) or video (MP4, MOV, MKV, AVI,
        WebM - the audio track is used), up to 50 MB. Everything is converted to
        16 kHz mono. Files longer than 10 minutes are cut to their first 10 minutes.
        Clips under 0.25 s or pure silence are rejected; clips under 2 s get a warning.

Step 2  SPEECH CHECKS (out-of-distribution, "OOD")
        The app checks that the input looks like speech (not music, tones, noise, long
        silence or very quiet audio). If it fails, the verdict is still shown but with a
        warning "This doesn't look like normal speech" - the verdict is much less reliable.

Step 3  WHOLE-FILE SCORE  (this decides the verdict)
        The speech region is cut into 4-second windows (E7a's training crop) every 1 s.
        Each window gets a logit (raw score); the file score is the MEAN window logit.
        That score passes through E7a's calibration (fitted on ASVspoof dev) to give a
        calibrated probability P(fake).
            P(fake) >= 50%  ->  SYNTHETIC
            P(fake) <  50%  ->  REAL
            P(real) = 1 - P(fake)
            Confidence = max(P(fake), P(real))   (the model's belief in its own call,
                                                  NOT a measured accuracy)

Step 4  TIME-LOCAL ANALYSIS  (display only - never changes the verdict)
        4-second windows are slid across the whole recording every 0.5 s (the hop grows
        for long files so that at most 120 windows are scored). Windows without enough
        speech energy are skipped ("No speech"). Each timeline step receives the mean
        log-odds of the speech windows covering its centre, converted back to a
        percentage. Consecutive steps at or above 50% are merged into FLAGGED SEGMENTS
        (S1, S2, ...), each with a severity:
            High   = mean synthetic probability >= 85%
            Medium = 70-85%
            Low    = 50-70%
        Because windows are 4 s long, segment boundaries are accurate to about +/- 2 s.

Step 5  MODEL-FREE ACOUSTIC MEASUREMENTS  (display only)
        Signal-processing measurements of the whole file (energy above 4 kHz, effective
        bandwidth, spectral flux, high-band noisiness, pitch variation, pitch jitter,
        pause noise floor) are compared against 1,700 real reference recordings
        (ASVspoof train/dev bona fide, clean and degraded with codecs, noise, telephone).
        Values below the 5th or above the 95th percentile are reported as "unusual".
        These never change the verdict; they help a human understand the audio.

Step 6  TRUST LOOKUP
        The app looks up the "confidence band" of this verdict (distance of P(fake) from
        50%) in a table built by scripts/evaluation/error_analysis.py from E7a's stored scores on
        held-out test sets, and reports how often verdicts in that band were right.

            Band        P(fake) range              Trust level
            0           40-60%                     very low
            1           30-40% or 60-70%           low
            2           20-30% or 70-80%           medium
            3           10-20% or 80-90%           high
            4           <=10% or >=90%             very high

Step 7  EXPLANATION AND REPORT
        Plain-language reasoning lines, per-segment evidence and a downloadable HTML
        report are generated.

Typical speed: seconds per minute of audio on the GPU (RTX 3050), about 40 s to 3 min
per minute on a laptop CPU; plus ~15 s the first time while the model loads.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. PAGES AND FEATURES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The sidebar has two sections. The app opens on Detect.

USE
  Detect        The main tool. Upload an audio or video file (up to 50 MB).
                A stepper shows progress through the steps. You first get a PREVIEW
                (file card + Spectrogram Lab) and press "Analyze recording". A real
                progress bar runs; then the full result appears (section 4).
                Sidebar: "Use the GPU" toggle, the device in use, and the limitations.
                If the GPU runs out of memory the app automatically falls back to CPU.
                "Download report (HTML)" saves a self-contained report.

  Compare A/B   Load a known/trusted voice (A, cyan) and a suspected recording (B,
                amber). Both are drawn on one time axis with linked or independent
                playback, measured the same way, and optionally run through the
                detector. Buttons: Swap A <-> B, Reset, Replace file.

  Batch         Upload many audio files and/or ZIP archives. Each file becomes one row:
                verdict, synthetic %, real %, confidence %, trust level, duration,
                whether it was cut to 10 min, OOD flag and reasons, flagged segments.
                Summary stat cards (files / called SYNTHETIC / called REAL / errors) and
                a "Download CSV" button.

  History       Every analysis made in this browser tab (Detect, Compare), newest first,
                with stat cards (analyses / SYNTHETIC / REAL / near the 50% line),
                a table, CSV download and "Clear history". Lost on reload.

RESEARCH  (reads experiments/ only; never loads PyTorch, so it opens fast)
  Results         Test results on 100 real + 100 fake ASVspoof 2019 eval clips: accuracy, F1,
                  precision, recall, mistakes per 100 and missed fakes by system, as bar
                  charts, plus the large held-out test figures.
  Error analysis  Where E7a goes wrong on held-out data and when to trust a verdict.

4. GRAPHS AND PANELS ON THE DETECT PAGE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The result is shown top to bottom in this order.

4.1  FINAL VERDICT PANEL
     What it shows:  A large ring gauge (confidence %), the word REAL (green) or
                     SYNTHETIC (red), a sentence with both probabilities, a
                     REAL/SYNTHETIC split meter, and a facts column (model, confidence,
                     held-out trust chip, analysis time, duration).
     The split meter: a horizontal bar, green part = P(real), red part = P(fake), with a
                     marker at the 50% decision line. The side that is larger wins.
     How to read it: The ring is the model's confidence in its OWN call. A 95% ring on
                     REAL means P(fake) = 5%. Confidence is not accuracy - check the
                     trust chip and the Reliability panel next.

4.2  AI MODEL VERDICT CARD (left)
     A smaller ring showing the SYNTHETIC probability, two horizontal bars
     ("REAL speech probability" and "SYNTHETIC speech probability"), and a stats row:
     classification, confidence, decision rule (SYNTHETIC at >= 50%), and how many
     4-s windows were scored. A sentence explains which side of the 50% line the score
     fell on. Footer shows the device (GPU name or CPU).

4.3  RELIABILITY PANEL - "How far to trust it" (right)
     What it shows:  The trust level (very low ... very high), an advice sentence, and
                     one bar per held-out test set:
                       - "Studio TTS, unseen attacks" (ASVspoof 2019 LA eval)
                       - "Internet audio" (In-the-Wild)
                     Each bar = accuracy (%) of E7a verdicts in the SAME confidence band
                     on that set, with the number of clips n.
     Colours:        green >= 90%, amber 70-90%, red < 70%.
     How to read it: This is the most honest number on the page. Measured values:

        Confidence band       ASVspoof eval accuracy   In-the-Wild accuracy
        very low (50+/-10%)   47.3%  (n=1,499)          52.1%  (n=909)
        low                   48.3%  (n=1,676)          53.3%  (n=1,067)
        medium                47.7%  (n=1,996)          58.6%  (n=1,263)
        high                  52.8%  (n=2,989)          67.9%  (n=2,055)
        very high (>=90/<=10) 87.7%  (n=63,077)         94.6%  (n=26,485)

     Analysis: only "very high" verdicts are dependable. Below 90% confidence the
     model is close to a coin flip on studio TTS, and only somewhat better on internet
     audio. If any set's accuracy is under 50%, the panel adds a warning that verdicts
     like this were usually WRONG on that kind of audio.

4.4  SPECTROGRAM LAB WITH SEGMENT ANALYSIS (interactive)
     What it shows:  A custom player with the waveform (top) and spectrogram (bottom) of the whole
                     recording on ONE shared time axis, plus the segment-level authenticity layer.
                     Segments come from the segment model (one prediction every 0.16 s, trained on
                     PartialSpoof's time-stamped labels). Every segment is exactly one of:
                             REAL   P(real) >= P(fake)  (a 50/50 tie is REAL)   green
                             FAKE   P(fake) >  P(real)                          red
                             NO SPEECH  silence (no speech energy)              grey
                     There is no uncertain class: a 51% / 49% speech segment still gets the winning
                     label. Grey marks silence only; it is not a real/fake judgement.
                       - SEGM. strip above the views: one coloured cell per segment.
                       - The waveform is coloured by its segment, on a faint matching background.
                       - The spectrogram carries a transparent green / red overlay (the frequency
                         pattern stays readable) with a coloured edge on top.
                       - White vertical lines where the classification changes. Time markers below.
                       - Hover anywhere: time, frequency, level and the segment's label and
                         confidence.
     SEGMENT ANALYSIS CARD (under the views)
                     - Status chip: "Mixed: real + fake", "Fake segments" or "All segments real".
                     - Authenticity timeline: adjacent green/red blocks [REAL][FAKE][REAL]... on the
                       recording's own time scale, with grey blocks for silence and no gaps. Hover = start/end, label,
                       confidence. CLICK = jump to that segment and play it.
                     - Stats: % and seconds of REAL and FAKE audio, seconds analysed, and the number
                       of REAL and FAKE segments.
                     - Segment breakdown list: "3.0-6.6 s  FAKE  99%" for every segment; clickable.
                     - While audio plays, the current segment is outlined in the strip, the
                       timeline and the list.
                     Confidence = the model's calibrated probability for the segment's class. It is
                     shown for information only and never changes the label. It is not an accuracy.
     Fallback:       without the segment model installed, segments are approximated from the
                     whole-clip model's 4-s windows (FAKE only above 50%); clips that fit in one window
                     get no segment labels, and blocks the old model never scored (silence) stay grey.
     Controls:       Play/Pause (Space), Zoom in/out/reset (+, -, 0), pan (arrow keys),
                     toggle Wave / Spectrum, frequency range (0-8 kHz full, 0-4 kHz
                     speech, 4-8 kHz high band, 0-1 kHz pitch), volume, playback speed.
                     Drag to select a region -> Play / Zoom / Clear the selection.
     How to read the spectrogram: x = time, y = frequency (low at bottom, up to 8 kHz),
                     brightness = energy. Natural speech shows clear harmonics (horizontal
                     stripes) during voiced sounds, formant bands, broadband bursts for
                     consonants, and some background noise in pauses. Things worth a
                     second look inside red segments: a hard ceiling where energy stops abruptly
                     (band-limited vocoder, resampling or a codec), unusually smooth / blurred
                     harmonics, buzzy or metallic high-band texture, perfectly black silent
                     pauses, or a sudden change of texture at a boundary line (possible splice).
                     None of these alone proves synthesis.
     Compare A/B:    both lanes get the same overlay after the detector runs (no card there).

4.5  EXPLAINABILITY CENTER - "Why was this audio flagged?"
     The waveform and spectrogram that used to sit here now live in the lab above, with the
     segment layer, so there is a single timeline on the page.

     d) MODEL PREDICTION SUMMARY (right panel)
        Final verdict, model, synthetic probability, confidence, flagged time
        ("x s of y s"), most-relied frequency band (only for spectrogram models; "-" for
        E7a), number of unusual measurements (out of 6), speech check passed/warning.
        "Read the full reasoning" expands the plain-language explanation.

     e) FREQUENCY ANALYSIS - average spectrum (LTAS, Long-Term Average Spectrum)
        x = frequency 0-8 kHz, y = average level in dB relative to the maximum
        (0 to -90 dB). Cyan filled curve. Amber dashed line at 4 kHz marks the start of
        the "high band".
        Reading: normal wideband speech has most energy below ~1 kHz, then falls off
        smoothly but still has measurable energy up to 7-8 kHz. Warning signs:
          - a cliff / brick-wall drop at 4 kHz, 5.5 kHz etc. -> band-limited vocoder,
            telephone channel, low-rate codec or upsampled audio;
          - a raised or flat noisy plateau above 4 kHz -> some neural vocoders add
            high-band hiss;
          - narrow spikes -> hum or tonal artefacts.
        Remember microphones, rooms and MP3 compression also change this curve.

     f) EVIDENCE BY FREQUENCY BAND (bar chart, shown only when the result has band
        saliency - spectrogram-input models. E7a reads WavLM features which have no
        frequency axis, so with the current model this chart is normally hidden.)
        For each band: cyan bar = share of model evidence, grey bar = share of mel
        bands. A cyan bar much longer than its grey bar means the model relied on that
        band more than its size would suggest.

     g) FEATURE ANALYSIS CARDS (six cards)
        Each card has a severity tag, a value, a sentence and a PERCENTILE GAUGE: a
        0-100 strip with the green zone between the 5th and 95th percentile of real
        reference speech and a dot for this file.
            Typical  (green) = inside 5th-95th percentile
            Unusual  (amber) = below 5th or above 95th
            Rare     (red)   = below 1st or above 99th
        "Details" expands an explanation and a secondary measurement.

        1. Spectral irregularities - spectral flux (frame-to-frame spectral change over
           32-ms frames). Low = over-smoothed spectra (typical of TTS acoustic models);
           high = abrupt jumps (possible splicing). Secondary: noisiness of 4-8 kHz band.
        2. Frequency characteristics - effective bandwidth in kHz (highest frequency
           within 50 dB of the peak). Low bandwidth = resampling, phone channel or
           low-rate vocoder.
        3. Temporal patterns - the segment authenticity timeline: REAL % and FAKE %, the
           block strip [REAL][FAKE]... (same segments as the lab), counts per class, and the
           full segment breakdown under "Details". Pitch variation and pitch jitter (flat
           intonation is common in synthetic speech) moved into "Details". Short clips show
           "Segment-level analysis unavailable".
        4. Energy distribution - % of speech energy above 4 kHz. Low = band-limited
           vocoder or heavy compression; high = added high-band hiss. Secondary: pause
           noise floor vs speech (reference only - MP3, noise suppression and editing
           also silence pauses, so it is never used in the explanation).
        5. Audio consistency - % of scored time at >= 50%, the range of local scores,
           and a MINI HISTOGRAM of timeline steps by synthetic score (10 bins, 0% left to
           100% right; green bins < 50%, red bins >= 50%).
              Consistent (green)     = no step crosses 50%
              Mixed (amber)          = some steps cross 50%
              Mostly flagged (red)   = half or more cross 50%
           Reading: a histogram piled at the left = consistently real; piled at the right
           = consistently synthetic; two humps = a recording that may be partly real and
           partly synthetic (e.g. spliced) - listen to the red segments.
        6. Model confidence - the confidence for the chosen class, plus a MINI BAR
           STRIP of the model's own 4-s file-level windows (hop 1 s) whose mean logit
           produced the file score. Tags: "Far from the line" (high/very high trust),
           "Moderate", "Near the line" (low/very low). Many bars on both sides of 50% =
           the file score is an average of disagreeing windows, so be careful.

4.6  SYNTHETIC PROBABILITY OVER TIME (main timeline chart, interactive)
     Axes:     x = time (s); y = synthetic score 0-100%, with the 50 tick labelled
               "50 > threshold".
     Layers:   - Background zones: red "SYNTHETIC zone" (above 50%), green "REAL zone" (50% and
                 below).
               - Grey dashed horizontal line at 50% = the decision threshold.
               - Blue smooth line = local synthetic probability over time; it breaks
                 (gaps) where there is no speech.
               - Dots: red when FAKE is the more probable class (above 50%), green otherwise.
               - Flagged segments: faint red rectangles, dashed red edges, labels S1, S2...
               - Hover anywhere: a vertical cursor and tooltip with time span, local
                 result and synthetic %.
     Resolution: one point every 0.5 s (longer for long files), each point covering a
               4-s window - so peaks are smoothed and boundaries are approximate (+/-2 s).
     How to read it:
               - Line flat and low in the green zone -> consistently real-looking.
               - Line flat and high in the red zone -> consistently synthetic-looking.
               - Line close to 50% -> the call in that region is weak (the label is still
                 REAL or FAKE; the low confidence shows in the number).
               - Line jumping from green to red and back -> partial manipulation is
                 possible (e.g. a synthetic sentence inserted into real speech), or the
                 model reacts to a change of noise/channel. Listen to that part.
               - The file verdict uses the whole-file windows of the speech region, so a
                 file can be SYNTHETIC with no single region above 50%, or REAL with a
                 few flagged regions.
     If the clip fits in one window (very short), the chart has a single value and a
     note asks for a longer recording.

4.7  FLAGGED SEGMENTS
     A table: segment, start, end, duration, synthetic score (shown as a progress bar),
     peak %, severity, main evidence. Below it one expander per segment with:
       - an AUDIO PLAYER for exactly that segment (listen to it!),
       - "Model evidence" - why the model flagged it,
       - "Acoustic measurements" - model-free comparisons of the segment against the rest
         of the recording and the reference (e.g. loudness, brightness, pitch), stated as
         "possible readings, not proof".
     If nothing crosses 50% the panel says so; if the verdict is SYNTHETIC with no
     segments, the evidence is spread over the whole file.

4.8  ADVANCED FORENSICS (expanders)
     a) Why: full explanation - all reasoning lines in plain language.

     b) Forensic view: spectrogram and saliency (static 3-panel figure)
        Top    - waveform coloured by local analysis (red suspected fake, green real,
                 grey no speech).
        Middle - 128-band log-mel spectrogram (frequency on a mel scale, so low
                 frequencies get more room) with a region bar on top showing the
                 Real/Fake/No-speech regions.
        Bottom - local synthetic probability over time together with the model's own
                 4-s file-level windows, against the 50% line.
        Note: E7a has no frequency-band saliency (WavLM features have no frequency axis).

     c) Whole-file acoustic measurements compared with real speech (table)
        Measurement, value, unit, percentile among real speech, reading
        (normal / unusual (low) / unusual (high) / n/a). Same data as the feature cards.

     d) Inside the model: Grad-CAM, attention over time, WavLM layers
        (turn on "Compute"; works on the DECISIVE window = the most fake window if the
        verdict is SYNTHETIC, the most real window if REAL)
        - GRAD-CAM HEATMAP: x = time frames, y = the model's 128 learned features (NOT
          frequency). Bright areas pushed the score towards SYNTHETIC. Bright vertical
          columns = specific moments that drove the decision.
        - ATTENTION OVER TIME: line plot of the attention-pooling weight across the
          window; dashed line = uniform weight. Peaks above the line are the moments the
          model weighted most when summarising the window; a flat curve means it used the
          whole window evenly.
        - WAVLM LAYER WEIGHTS: bar chart of how much the detector mixes each WavLM layer
          (0 = convolutional encoder output, higher = deeper transformer layers); dashed
          line = uniform weight. Lower layers carry acoustic/signal detail, higher layers
          more phonetic/linguistic information. This is a property of the trained model,
          the same for every file.
        These show where the model looked, not proof of manipulation.

4.9  DOWNLOADABLE HTML REPORT
     Self-contained page with: verdict box and split meter, trust panel, synthetic-
     probability-over-time image, flagged-segment table with evidence, full reasoning,
     the forensic-view figure, the measurement table, and the limitations text.

5. GRAPHS ON THE COMPARE A/B PAGE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  SIDE-BY-SIDE SIGNAL VIEW
     The Spectrogram Lab with two lanes (A cyan, B amber) on one time axis. "Linked"
     plays, seeks and selects both together; "Independent" lets each lane play alone
     (per-lane play buttons). After the detector runs, each lane gets its local-score
     strip and flagged segments. Reading: compare harmonic structure, bandwidth ceiling,
     background noise and pause texture of the trusted voice against the suspect.

5.2  MODEL VERDICT CARDS (A and B)
     For each recording: ring of synthetic %, REAL and SYNTHETIC bars, verdict chip,
     confidence and held-out trust level. Run with "Run the detector on A and B".
     Results are shared with Detect and History.

5.3  FEATURE COMPARISON CARDS (six cards, A bar vs B bar)
     RMS energy (dBFS)       - average loudness (bar scaled -60..0 dBFS).
     Spectral centroid (Hz)  - "brightness", centre of mass of the spectrum.
     Spectral roll-off (Hz)  - frequency below which 85% of energy lies.
     Zero-crossing rate      - how often the waveform crosses zero: noisiness.
     Spectral flatness (0-1) - noise-like (1) vs tonal (0).
     Crest factor (dB)       - peak above average level: dynamics / compression.
     Each card shows the difference ("B - A: +3.1 dB" or "B vs A: +12%").
     Reading: large differences in centroid/roll-off often mean different bandwidth or
     processing; much lower crest factor suggests heavy compression or limiting. But
     microphones, rooms and codecs also cause these - they are not evidence on their own.

5.4  MFCC PROFILE (line chart)
     x = MFCC coefficient 0-12, y = mean value over the recording; one line per
     recording (A cyan, B amber). MFCCs summarise the spectral envelope (timbre).
     Coefficient 0 tracks overall level; 1-3 the broad spectral tilt; higher ones finer
     detail. Reading: two lines that track closely = similar timbre/channel; large gaps
     at low coefficients = different tilt (mic, channel, codec or a different voice
     model); differences only at high coefficients are usually minor.

5.5  COMPARISON SUMMARY
     Auto-generated sentences: duration difference, loudness difference, centroid and
     roll-off difference in %, whether the detector gives the same verdict to both, and a
     reminder that recording conditions also cause differences. "Exact values" gives the
     raw numbers.

     Choose a test set (ASVspoof eval, In-the-Wild). Stat cards: clips, accuracy, real
     called SYNTHETIC (false rejects), fakes called REAL (false accepts).

        Set             Clips    Accuracy   Real->SYNTHETIC   Fake->REAL
        ASVspoof eval   71,237   83.3%      0.1%              18.6%
        In-the-Wild     31,779   88.9%      6.7%              18.6%

     a) "WHEN TO TRUST A VERDICT" - bar chart
        x = confidence band (very low ... very high), y = accuracy 0-100%.
        This is the table the Detect page's trust panel looks up (numbers in 4.3).
        Analysis: the model is very good when it is very sure, and the vast majority of
        clips fall in the very-high band (63,077 of 71,237 on ASVspoof eval). Everything

     e) PER RECORDING (table, when available) - per-recording P(fake) as a progress bar.

     f) WORST MISTAKES (two tabs) - fakes called REAL with the lowest P(fake) (false
        accepts) and real clips called FAKE with the highest P(fake) (false rejects).
        Useful for listening to what fools the model.

7. HOW TO READ A RESULT - CHECKLIST
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Verdict and split meter: REAL or SYNTHETIC, and how far from 50%.
  2. Trust panel: is it "very high"? If not, treat the verdict as a weak hint.
  3. Speech-check warning? If yes, the verdict is much less reliable.
  4. Timeline: is the line consistently on one side, or does it cross the line?
  5. Flagged segments: LISTEN to every one. Check its severity and evidence.
  6. Spectrogram Lab / LTAS: look for bandwidth cliffs, smoothed harmonics, odd
     high-band texture, silent pauses, texture changes at segment edges.
  7. Feature cards: count "Unusual"/"Rare" tags, remembering codecs and microphones
     cause many of them too.
  8. If you have a known-real sample of the same speaker, use Compare A/B.
  9. Never use one verdict as the only evidence about a real person.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. LIMITATIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Research prototype and screening aid, not proof. Every verdict can be wrong.
  - Trained on English studio speech from 2019 (ASVspoof 2019 LA, attacks A01-A06).
  - Misses about 1 in 5 fakes on both ASVspoof eval and In-the-Wild, and about 3 in 4
    modern voice-conversion fakes (DEEP-VOICE windows, e.g. RVC-style conversion).
  - Calls 0.1% of real ASVspoof studio recordings and 6.7% of real In-the-Wild clips
    SYNTHETIC.
  - Heavily processed audio (strong compression, noise suppression, phone lines) can
    shift the score either way.
  - Flagged regions are model-flagged suspicions; acoustic measurements are
    descriptions, not proof of manipulation.
  - Not for legal, journalistic, financial or personal-safety decisions on its own, and
    not for surveillance or judging individuals.
